PhD Candidate · Chemical Engineering · UC Davis

Siddharth Sonti

I build computational methods that let atomistic simulations reach the timescales that matter — and use them to understand catalysts and design processes for a more sustainable future.

I am co-advised by Prof. Surl-Hee (Shirley) Ahn and Prof. Ambarish Kulkarni in the Department of Chemical Engineering at the University of California, Davis. My work sits at the intersection of enhanced sampling, machine-learning potentials and molecular dynamics, with applications to catalysis, hydrogen production and carbon capture. I am the developer of ParGaMD, a parallelizable Gaussian accelerated MD method for fast free-energy exploration.

01

Research

Enhanced sampling

Can we push atomistic simulations to longer timescales? I develop and apply methods such as Gaussian accelerated MD, weighted ensemble and metadynamics — including ParGaMD, which parallelizes GaMD for HPC clusters.

ML potentials & catalysis

Computational workflows built on machine-learning interatomic potentials to study catalyst behavior — from zeolite-confined gold nanoclusters to the mechanisms of zeolite formation.

Sustainable processes

Theoretical process development for sustainability needs, including hydrogen production, hydrogen carriers and carbon capture.
